MEDICATION REFILL POLICY
Effective Date: January 1, 2026
Department: Pharmacy Services & Clinical Operations
Regulatory Basis: Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 | NDPS Act, 1985 | CDSCO Guidelines

OVERVIEW
This policy governs the process for requesting and approving medication refills at this
healthcare facility in accordance with the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940, the Narcotic Drugs
and Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act, 1985, and guidelines issued by the Central Drugs
Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO). The policy ensures patient safety, regulatory
compliance, and continuity of care.

HOW TO REQUEST A MEDICATION REFILL
Patients may request refills through the following channels:
1. Patient Portal: Log in at myhealth.healthsystem.in and navigate to "Medications" →
   "Request Refill." Processing time: 2–3 working days.
2. Pharmacy Counter: Visit the in-facility pharmacy with your previous prescription.
3. Telemedicine Consultation: Request renewal during a scheduled telemedicine or in-person
   appointment with a Registered Medical Practitioner (RMP).
4. Helpline: Call 1800-123-4567 (Toll Free) Monday–Saturday, 9am–6pm.

STANDARD REFILL CRITERIA
A medication refill will be approved when:
- The patient has a valid, current prescription from a registered medical practitioner.
- The prescribed medication is not a Schedule X or NDPS-listed substance.
- The patient is not overdue for a mandatory clinical review or lab investigation.
- The quantity requested does not exceed the originally authorised supply.

SCHEDULE H AND SCHEDULE H1 DRUG REFILLS
Under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940, Schedule H and H1 drugs require a valid prescription
for every dispensing:
- Schedule H drugs (e.g., antibiotics, antihypertensives): Refill requires a valid prescription.
  Repeat prescriptions may be issued by the treating RMP after clinical review.
- Schedule H1 drugs (e.g., third-generation cephalosporins, carbapenems): Require a fresh
  prescription for every refill. These cannot be refilled on the basis of an older prescription.
- Over-the-counter (OTC) medications not listed in Schedule H may be dispensed without
  prescription but only in standard therapeutic quantities.

NDPS ACT MEDICATIONS — SPECIAL RESTRICTIONS
Medications listed under the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act, 1985
(e.g., morphine, oxycodone, tramadol, alprazolam, diazepam) are subject to strict controls:
- NDPS medications require a special NDPS prescription form as mandated by the relevant
  State Excise/Drugs Control Authority.
- Refills require an in-person consultation with the prescribing doctor. Telemedicine
  prescriptions for NDPS substances are not permitted under any circumstances.
- Lost or misplaced NDPS prescriptions cannot be replaced without a fresh clinical evaluation.
- Early refill requests for NDPS medications will be denied unless accompanied by a written
  clinical justification from the treating RMP.

PHARMACY COORDINATION AND GENERIC SUBSTITUTION
- This facility coordinates with empanelled pharmacy partners for home delivery of approved
  medications under Jan Aushadhi Scheme where applicable.
- Generic substitution is permitted in accordance with the NMC's guidelines on generic
  prescriptions, unless the prescribing doctor has documented a specific clinical reason for
  brand prescription.
- Patients are encouraged to use Jan Aushadhi Kendras for significant cost savings on generics.

SITUATIONS REQUIRING A FRESH CLINICAL APPOINTMENT
Refill will not be processed without a fresh consultation in the following cases:
- The patient has not visited the prescribing doctor in more than 6 months (chronic conditions)
  or 3 months (psychiatric medications).
- Recent lab investigations are required to ensure safety (e.g., HbA1c for metformin,
  LFT for statins, renal function for ACE inhibitors).
- The patient reports adverse drug reactions or new symptoms.
- A dosage change or new medication is required.

MEDICATION DISPOSAL
Unused or expired medications should be deposited at designated drug take-back counters
available at the facility pharmacy. Do not discard medications in household waste or flush
them, as this violates the Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016.
