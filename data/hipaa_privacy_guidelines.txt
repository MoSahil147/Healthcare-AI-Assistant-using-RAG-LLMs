PATIENT DATA PRIVACY GUIDELINES
Effective Date: January 1, 2026
Department: Compliance & Privacy Office
Regulatory Basis: Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 | IT Act, 2000 | NMC Regulations

OVERVIEW
This document explains how this healthcare facility collects, stores, processes, and protects
your personal and health data in accordance with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023
(DPDPA), the Information Technology Act, 2000, and the Electronic Health Records (EHR) Standards
notified by the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW). Your privacy is a fundamental
right protected under Article 21 of the Constitution of India.

WHAT IS PERSONAL HEALTH DATA?
Personal health data (also called health-related personal data) includes any information that
can directly or indirectly identify you in the context of your medical care:
- Name, date of birth, Aadhaar number, mobile number, or address linked to your health records
- Clinical notes, diagnosis codes (ICD-10), prescription history, lab reports, and imaging
- Insurance details, ABHA (Ayushman Bharat Health Account) ID, and billing information
- Biometric data collected during registration or telemedicine verification
- Data transmitted through the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM) ecosystem

YOUR RIGHTS UNDER THE DPDPA, 2023
As a data principal (patient), you have the following rights under the DPDPA, 2023:

Right to Access Information: You may request details about what personal data we hold about
you and how it is being processed. Requests must be submitted in writing or through the patient
portal. We will respond within 30 days.

Right to Correction and Erasure: You may request correction of inaccurate data or erasure of
data that is no longer necessary for the purpose for which it was collected, subject to legal
retention obligations (medical records must be retained for a minimum of 3 years as per MoHFW
EHR standards; 5 years for surgical records).

Right to Grievance Redressal: You may raise a grievance with our Data Protection Officer (DPO)
and, if unresolved, escalate to the Data Protection Board of India constituted under the DPDPA.

Right to Nominate: You may nominate another individual to exercise your data rights in the
event of your death or incapacity.

Right to Withdraw Consent: Where processing is based on your consent, you may withdraw
consent at any time. Withdrawal will not affect the lawfulness of prior processing.

HOW WE USE YOUR HEALTH DATA
Your health data may be used without additional consent for:
- Treatment: Sharing with treating doctors, specialists, diagnostic laboratories, and pharmacies
  involved in your care.
- Payment and Insurance: Sharing with your TPA (Third-Party Administrator), insurance company,
  or government scheme administrators (e.g., PMJAY/Ayushman Bharat) for claims processing.
- Healthcare Operations: Internal quality audits, staff training, accreditation (NABH), and
  public health reporting mandated by law.
- ABDM Integration: Sharing through the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission Health Information
  Exchange (HIE) with your explicit consent via ABHA-linked consent artefacts.

All other uses require your written, informed consent.

DATA SECURITY MEASURES
This facility implements the following safeguards as required under the IT Act, 2000 and the
DPDPA, 2023:
- End-to-end encryption for all electronic health records and telemedicine data
- Role-based access control (RBAC) ensuring only authorised clinical staff access patient data
- Audit trails for all access to sensitive health records
- Data localisation — all health data is stored on servers located within India
- Annual information security audits in compliance with ISO 27001 standards

BREACH NOTIFICATION
In the event of a personal data breach, affected patients will be notified as required by the
DPDPA, 2023 and the CERT-In guidelines. Notification will include the nature of the breach,
data categories affected, likely consequences, and remedial steps taken.

SHARING DATA WITH GOVERNMENT AUTHORITIES
Health data may be disclosed to government authorities including the MoHFW, ICMR, state health
departments, or courts of law when required by a legal obligation, without patient consent.
