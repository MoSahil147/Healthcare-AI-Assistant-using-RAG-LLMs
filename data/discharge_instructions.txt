PATIENT DISCHARGE INSTRUCTIONS
Effective Date: January 1, 2026
Department: Inpatient Care & Case Management
Regulatory Basis: Clinical Establishments Act, 2010 | NMC Code of Medical Ethics, 2002

OVERVIEW
These discharge instructions are issued to patients leaving this facility following inpatient
admission or day-care surgical procedures, in compliance with the Clinical Establishments
(Registration and Regulation) Act, 2010 and NABH (National Accreditation Board for Hospitals)
standards. Please read carefully and retain this document for your recovery period.

GENERAL RECOVERY GUIDELINES
- Rest is essential for the first 48–72 hours following discharge. Avoid strenuous physical
  activity unless explicitly cleared by your treating doctor.
- Do not drive or operate machinery for at least 24 hours after receiving general anaesthesia
  or sedative medications.
- Arrange for a responsible adult caregiver to accompany you home and stay with you overnight
  following any procedure involving anaesthesia.
- Follow the diet chart provided by the facility's dietitian. Most post-operative patients
  should begin with liquids and advance to a soft diet as tolerated.
- Maintain adequate hydration. Unless fluid restriction is prescribed, aim for 2–2.5 litres
  of water daily.

WOUND CARE INSTRUCTIONS
If you have a surgical incision or wound:
- Keep the wound clean and dry for the first 24–48 hours or as directed by your surgeon.
- Dressing changes must be performed as instructed. Wash hands thoroughly before and after.
- Do not immerse the wound in water (no bathing in tubs, swimming pools) until your doctor
  confirms adequate wound healing, typically at the first follow-up visit.
- Minor redness and swelling around the wound site is normal in the initial days post-surgery.

WARNING SIGNS — SEEK EMERGENCY CARE IMMEDIATELY IF YOU EXPERIENCE:
- Fever above 38.5°C (101.3°F) persisting beyond 24 hours
- Increasing redness, warmth, swelling, foul-smelling discharge, or pus at the wound site
- Wound edges separating or opening (wound dehiscence)
- Severe or worsening pain not controlled by prescribed analgesics
- Breathlessness, chest pain, or rapid heartbeat (possible pulmonary embolism)
- Swelling, redness, or pain in the calf or leg (possible deep vein thrombosis)
- Sudden confusion, slurred speech, or difficulty waking (possible neurological event)
- Inability to retain oral fluids for more than 24 hours post-discharge

If any of the above occur, call 112 (National Emergency Number) or proceed immediately to
the nearest government or empanelled private hospital emergency department.
Do not wait for a scheduled follow-up appointment in these cases.

MEDICATION INSTRUCTIONS POST-DISCHARGE
- Take all prescribed medicines exactly as directed on the discharge prescription.
- Complete the full course of antibiotics even if you feel better before the course ends.
  Premature discontinuation contributes to antimicrobial resistance (AMR) as flagged by the
  Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR).
- Pain management: Use prescribed analgesics first. Paracetamol (acetaminophen) 500mg–1g
  may be used as directed. Avoid NSAIDs such as ibuprofen without medical advice if you have
  a history of gastric ulcers, kidney disease, or are on blood thinners.
- Avoid alcohol completely while on antibiotics, opioid analgesics, or sedatives.
- A complete medication list with doses, timings, and duration is provided in your discharge
  summary. Retain this summary — it will be required at follow-up visits.

FOLLOW-UP APPOINTMENT SCHEDULE
A post-discharge follow-up must be scheduled within the timeframe indicated on your discharge
summary. Standard timelines are:
- General surgery follow-up: 7–10 days post-discharge
- Cardiac procedure follow-up: 5–7 days post-discharge
- Orthopaedic surgery follow-up: 10–14 days post-discharge
- Obstetric/gynaecological procedures: 7 days post-discharge

To book your follow-up, call 1800-123-4567 or log in to the patient portal at
myhealth.healthsystem.in and select "Book Appointment."

ACTIVITY RESTRICTIONS
Unless your treating physician has provided different instructions:
- No lifting objects heavier than 5 kg for 2 weeks following abdominal or thoracic surgery.
- Light walking starting from the day after discharge is encouraged to prevent DVT.
- Do not drive until you are completely off narcotic or sedative pain medications and your
  reaction time has normalised.
- Return-to-work timelines vary by procedure; clarify with your surgeon at the follow-up visit.
